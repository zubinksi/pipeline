How to read this

First tests in people, mainly for safety.

Does it work, and at what dose?

Large trials that test whether it works well enough to approve. The last step before applying.

Many drugs stop along the way. Even final-stage drugs are usually a few years from pharmacies.

Watching for

A short, hand-picked list of results that would change the picture. Dates are when a trial is expected to finish collecting data (they often slip); results usually follow within a few months.

Heart & cholesterol

LDL is the "bad" cholesterol that statins lower; the LDL drugs here do it without being a statin or a daily pill. Lp(a) is a separate, mostly inherited cholesterol particle that diet and statins barely touch; no Lp(a) drug is approved yet. Lowering either isn't automatically the same as preventing heart attacks: "Prevents heart attacks" means a large trial showed fewer heart attacks and strokes.

Obesity & metabolism

Weight loss is the average drop in body weight in the main trials, usually over about a year and a half, on top of diet and exercise advice. Trials differ, so close numbers aren't a real difference. "Prevents heart attacks" means a large trial showed fewer heart attacks and strokes, not just weight loss.

Alzheimer's & the brain

These drugs clear amyloid, a protein that builds up in the brain in Alzheimer's. "Slowing of decline" is how much slower memory and daily function got worse than on placebo, in early-stage patients over about 18 months. None stop or reverse the disease, and brain swelling or bleeding (ARIA) is a known risk.

Cancer

Cancer drugs are usually judged first on whether they hold off the cancer's growth for longer ("progression"), and then on whether people live longer. Each drug here is for a specific cancer, often only with a certain gene change.
